# Commercial licensing

TerraForge is free — genuinely free, source and all — for noncommercial use,
under the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE). Commercial use
needs a paid licence from Geekatplay Studio.

This page says where the line is, in plain language. The [LICENSE](LICENSE)
file is what governs; this page is how we read it.

## First, the part people worry about

**Whatever you make with TerraForge is yours.** Terrains, heightmaps,
renders, exported meshes, material libraries, scene files — we claim no
rights in them under either licence, and we never will. A licence on a tool
is not a claim on the work.

The licence is about **who may run the software**, not about what comes out
of it.

## Free — no licence needed, no email needed

- Learning the software, following tutorials, experimenting.
- Personal and hobby projects, art you make for yourself or for friends.
- Students, coursework, theses, and teaching at any school or university.
- Charities, public research bodies, public safety and health organisations,
  environmental organisations, and government institutions — named in the
  licence itself, free regardless of how they are funded.
- Reading, modifying and rebuilding the source; sharing your modified build,
  as long as it travels with the same licence and the `Required Notice:`
  line.
- Publishing images you made while learning, including on a personal site,
  a portfolio, or social media, with no strings.

## Needs a commercial licence

- Work you are paid for: a client render, a commission, a contract.
- Film, television, games, advertising, architecture, simulation — any
  production, in-house or freelance.
- Selling what you make with it: renders, prints, assets, asset packs,
  game content, stock imagery, courses built around production output.
- Use inside a for-profit company, by employees or contractors, even for
  internal or R&D purposes.
- Bundling TerraForge, or a build of it, into a product or a paid service.

If money is involved anywhere in the chain, assume a licence is needed and
ask. Asking is free and the answer is usually quick.

## Questions we get asked

**I am a hobbyist and I sold one print. Am I in trouble?**
No. Talk to us — that is a conversation, not an enforcement action. The
licence itself gives 32 days to put things right after any written notice,
and we would rather sell you a licence than argue with you.

**I make monetised tutorial videos about TerraForge.**
Teaching the software is welcome and free, including on a monetised channel.
Producing paid client work on camera is not.

**I am a freelancer building a portfolio to get hired.**
Free while it is a portfolio. The moment it is billed work, it is
commercial.

**I want to evaluate it before my studio buys.**
Ask for a written evaluation licence. There is no automatic trial period
built into the licence, but we grant evaluation licences readily.

**I am a nonprofit that charges for some services.**
The licence covers nonprofit organisations by category, not by revenue
model. If you are unsure where you sit, ask and we will confirm in writing.

**Can I fork it, or ship a plugin?**
Yes — modify it, fork it, share the fork, all noncommercially, keeping the
licence and the notice line intact. A commercial fork or a commercially
distributed plugin needs a licence.

## Getting a licence

Contact Geekatplay Studio through the form at
[terraforge.art](https://terraforge.art) or write to
`licensing@terraforge.art`. Tell us:

- who you are (individual, studio, company) and roughly how many people
  would use it,
- what you would use it for,
- whether you need it on more than one machine.

Pricing is per case at this stage — TerraForge is early, and a solo
freelancer and a forty-seat studio should not pay the same. Written
confirmation comes back to you, so you have something to file.

## Why this licence

TerraForge is built by one person and given away to everyone who wants to
learn landscapes. The noncommercial licence keeps it that way: free for
students, hobbyists, teachers and nonprofits forever, while the studios that
earn from it help pay for its development. It is a paid-for-profit,
free-for-everyone-else arrangement, and it is deliberate.

If you want to support the project without needing a commercial licence,
there is [a coffee fund](https://geekatplay.gumroad.com/coffee).

## A note on the words

TerraForge is **source-available**, not open source. "Open source", as the
term is formally defined, means no restriction on the field of use —
including commercial use — so a noncommercial licence does not qualify, and
we do not claim the label. The source is public, readable, forkable and
modifiable; commercial use is the one thing that costs money.
